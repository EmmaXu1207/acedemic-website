Jiayi Xu (Emma) is a Master's student in the Department of Physics at the University of Toronto who works on multi-object spectrograph development and balloon-borne telescope instrumentation. Her current research focuses on optical system design, calibration, and performance evaluation, as well as synthetic photometry studies for instrument characterization and planning. She plays an important role in bridging engineering designs with scientific objectives by building tools like exposure time calculators to help evaluate system capability and optimize science target selection. She completed her Honours Bachelor of Science with High Distinction at the University of Toronto with the department of Astronomy, where she gained experience in statistical analysis, advanced programming, hands-on experiments, and theoretical studies on early-Universe evolution. Her interest in optical astronomical instrumentation fuels her passion to further her graduate education.

Research Projects

DMD-MOS

Digital Micro-mirror Device based Multi-Object Spectrograph

DMD-MOS website

My work: Assembly, Optical Alignment, Calibration, System Performance Tests, Sky Tests.

✨ DMD-MOS used a DMD to instantly configure multi-slits masks for the incoming telescope field, thus minimizing telescope overhead and facilitating efficient observing for hundreds of targets, which is an absolute game changer of next generation multi-objects spectrograph.✨

Project website: DMD-MOS website

Abandoning the traditional approaches to multi-object spectrographs, DMD-MOS (Digital Micro-mirror Device-based Multi-Object Spectrograph) leverages a robust, elegant approach for mask creation. DMDs are arrays of microscopic flat mirrors where each micro-mirror can be addressed to selectively flip between two discrete angles. With this in mind, we have designed two optical channels for DMD-MOS - one for each of these two discrete positions: 1) an imaging channel that can be used for multi-object target selection and 2) a spectrograph channel. By leveraging this feature, the DMD-MOS can simultaneously image the field of view (FOV) and disperse the selected targets. In this work, we present the results of our laboratory testing of DMD-MOS including spectral resolution, line spread function, wavelength coverage, and the mapping between the DMD and imaging channel planes, the latter of which enables DMD-MOS and our control software to instantaneously configure the DMD to observe the chosen target(s) in the acquisition field.
